## The problem

When a reactor misbehaved, the engineers who remembered the last time it happened were retiring. The institutional record existed — six decades of maintenance logs, incident reports and shift handovers — but it was scanned, handwritten in parts, and split across Dutch, French and English.

## What we did

We digitised and structured 12,000 documents, then built retrieval that an engineer can query in plain language during an incident and get back the specific page, with the original scan alongside the transcription so they can check it themselves.

We were explicit about the failure mode: handwriting recognition on 1970s carbon copies is imperfect, and a confidently wrong maintenance instruction is dangerous. Every answer shows its source image.

## Outcome

12,000 documents searchable in under a second. In its first quarter, the archive shortened two unplanned outages by pointing engineers at fixes applied in 1994 and 2008.
